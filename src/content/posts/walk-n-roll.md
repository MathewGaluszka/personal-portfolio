For our final project of 4 in my first year design project course, we had the opportunity to meet members of the community, listen and learn about their day to day life with chronic illness and build a device based around their needs. As a part of this 2 month project, we met Kevin, who required a mobility aid that bridged the gap between conventional walkers and wheelchairs, as he was right on the cusp of requiring both but didnt want to have to travel around with two seperate devices. Following a couple of design reviews, we ended up coming up with an idea for a 2 in 1 convertible walker and wheelchair that could be transitioned between the two options, alowing for greater mobility when he wanted it and extra support when needed.

![Early cardboard protoype, wheels would swing out and around to the rear](/images/cardboard.png)

Once we had a concept, I took on the fabrication of the full-scale model. Because it had to be built off-campus, this was a large part of the project, and my background in carpentry let me carry the design through to a working prototype. Building at full scale taught me how much a design has to account for real materials, tolerances, and assembly constraints, and it confirmed the value of physical prototyping for testing ideas.
