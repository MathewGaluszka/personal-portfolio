I have always loved niche art mediums, one of those being stained glass. However, getting custom pieces made can be quite costly. So when I came across youtube tutorials of how to make stained glass pieces for relatively cheap and without crazy expensive tools, I decided to give it a shot with my own engineering twist to it, and its turned into a hobby I quite enjoy. 

## Design and Fabrication Process

I first start out by creating a rough 2D sketch of how I want the panes to look in CAD software. This lets me see how all the lines flow into one another, and once I have a panel design set, I can tile it into 3D space to see if I like the proportions of the lamp as a whole. 

![CAD design of the first lamp I made](/images/lampcad.jpg)

Once I am happy with the overall design, I use a cricut machine to cut out stickers of the pattern, which I can then stick to my glass pieces to aid in cutting them out.

![Layout of all sticker pieces](/images/glassstickers.jpg)

Of course, glass cutting can be a finicky thing, and not every cut goes to plan. I had to deviate from the original drawing since I did not have enough glass, but I did manage to salvage the piece as a whole, pivoting to a white tip piece instead of the pink. I utilized a 3D printed panel form to line everything up and ensure that all the glass pieces were cut and grinded to the correct size.

![3D printed form to layout individual panels](/images/panelform.jpg)

After confirming fit, each piece is cleaned thoroughly and wrapped with a copper foil tape. This allows me to then solder everything together once everything is back and aligned in the form.

![Completed panels](/images/finishedpanels.jpg)

I then 3D print another jig to align everything in 3D space, and I then solder all the panels together.

![Panels aligned in 3D jig](/images/3djig.jpg)

With the shade done and final dimensions are known, I designed a lamp base in CAD and 3D printed that as well, routing the cables and light socket internally so that you wouldnt be able to see it. I printed this out of a special PLA that looks like metal once it is fully printed, and the effect turned out pretty nice. I also put in a Phillips Hue bulb, so I can control the lamp from my phone. I am also working on converting a Wii remote into a smart home remote so that I dont have to rely on my phone, and can instead have a physical controller for this and all future lamps I make, one of which is already in the works.
